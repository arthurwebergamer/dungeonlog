Rest day in Dungeonlog: pause without losing your streak

2026-11-06

Some days you just can't get through the list. Dungeonlog now has a rest day: you say you are taking today off, the monster of the day does no damage and your streak does not reset. In this article you will see how to turn rest on, what it changes and how to cancel it.

Where the button is

On the Tasks tab, next to the "+ Add" button, there is a button with a moon and the word "Rest". It only shows while the monster of the day is still standing: once you defeat the monster, the button disappears, because the day is already won.

What happens when you rest

When you tap "Rest", the app opens a sheet that asks "Rest today?" and lists four points, which are exactly what a rest day does:

  • no damage and no "You fell" screen;
  • your streak freezes, it does not reset;
  • today earns no XP or coins;
  • you can cancel anytime.

It is a clear trade: the day earns no rewards, but it does not charge you anything either. Tasks left undone on a rest day are not counted as a loss.

How the screen looks afterwards

Once you confirm, the button reads "Cancel rest" and the task counter is replaced by the message "Resting today — progress protected". Your tasks stay in the list, waiting.

Changed your mind? Tap "Cancel rest" and the day goes back to normal. Finishing a task also cancels the day's rest, so you can jump back into the game at any time without doing anything else.

Rest in the history and the streak

On the Stats tab, the month history shows every day. A rest day appears in blue, different from the won days, and the current streak keeps the same number as before: it is frozen, it neither goes up nor resets. In the capture below, the streak is 4 before and after day 10, which was marked as rest.

When to use it

The rest day is for days when you can't get through the list and don't want to be punished for it. It is not a way to skip the game every day, since a rest day earns no XP or coins. Dungeonlog is still a task app: rest just makes sure one bad day does not erase what you have built.
